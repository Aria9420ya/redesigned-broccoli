# Knowledge Map: COF Electrospun Proton Exchange Membranes

## Central Thesis

COF-based PEM research should be judged by whether ordered porous chemistry and electrospun scaffold architecture produce continuous, stable proton pathways that survive realistic PEMFC humidity, temperature, swelling, and MEA constraints.

## Main Mechanism Chain

COF chemistry -> acid/base/proton-site density -> water or carrier retention -> channel continuity -> conductivity -> swelling/mechanical tradeoff -> MEA performance.

## Key Research Axes

1. COF design: sulfonated, phosphonated, zwitterionic, imidazole/triazole, perfluoroalkyl-sulfonic, or heteropoly-acid anchored COFs.
2. Fiber scaffold: PAN, PVDF, PFSA-compatible fibers, aligned fibers, in situ COF growth, or mixed electrospinning.
3. Polymer matrix: Nafion/PFSA, SPEEK, PBI, PVDF-based composites, or other sulfonated polymers.
4. Operating window: hydrated low temperature, elevated temperature, low RH, anhydrous or quasi-anhydrous.
5. Translation: ex situ conductivity must be tied to swelling, durability, crossover, and MEA output.

## Frontier Questions

- Can COF channels be aligned or percolated along the membrane thickness?
- Can electrospun scaffolds improve conductivity and dimensional stability at the same time?
- Which proton carriers remain fixed after long hydration and fuel-cell operation?
- What COF loading crosses from channel-forming to aggregation/blocking?
- Why do some high-conductivity membranes underperform in MEA?

## Current Route Ranking

1. **PAN/PFSA/sulfonated COF sandwich route**: strongest fit to electrospinning + PEM stability; good first experimental route.
2. **PVDF/COF/ionic-liquid nanofiber route**: attractive for high-temperature work, but leaching and long-term durability need strict controls.
3. **Nafion/anchored heteropoly-acid COF route**: strong low-RH mechanism, useful if MEA performance is the main target.
4. **Continuous SCOF/support membrane route**: frontier benchmark; likely harder synthetically but important as a long-term target.

## Minimum Decision Logic

Choose a route only if it can answer all four questions:

- Where are the proton sites?
- Are they continuous across the membrane?
- What prevents swelling, leaching, or cracking?
- How will ex situ metrics connect to fuel-cell performance?
