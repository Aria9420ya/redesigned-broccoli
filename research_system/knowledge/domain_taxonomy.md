# Domain Taxonomy: Fuel-Cell Membrane Materials

## Level 1: Device Context

PEM fuel cells require membranes that conduct protons while blocking electrons and gases. A good membrane must balance conductivity, hydration, dimensional stability, mechanical strength, chemical durability, low gas crossover, and electrode compatibility.

## Level 2: Membrane Material Families

- PFSA membranes: Nafion and related ionomers; strong benchmark, high hydrated conductivity, expensive, humidity dependent.
- Hydrocarbon membranes: SPEEK, SPES, SPAES, PBI and derivatives; tunable chemistry, often need reinforcement or acid management.
- Composite membranes: polymer plus inorganic/organic fillers; aim to improve water retention, stability, conductivity, or crossover.
- Framework-based membranes: COF/MOF/CTF/porous organic networks; ordered channels and functional sites, but dispersion and brittleness are challenges.
- Electrospun membranes: nanofiber scaffolds or conductive fiber networks; useful for mechanical reinforcement, anisotropic pathways, and high interfacial area.

## Level 3: COF Roles In PEMs

- Proton channel: ordered pores decorated with acid/base groups.
- Water reservoir: hydrophilic pores retain water at lower RH.
- Acid immobilizer: anchors phosphoric acid, heteropoly acid, ionic liquids, or sulfonic/phosphonic groups.
- Mechanical modifier: reinforces or restrains swelling when integrated with fibers or porous supports.
- Crossover barrier: tortuous channels reduce methanol or gas crossover.

## Level 4: Electrospinning Roles

- Scaffold reinforcement: improves tensile strength and dimensional stability.
- Channel template: aligns polymer/COF domains or creates continuous nanofiber highways.
- Interface amplifier: increases contact area between COF, ionomer, and water domains.
- Thickness/morphology control: enables thin mats and layered sandwich membranes.

## Level 5: Key Tradeoffs

- More acid sites can increase conductivity but also swelling or leaching.
- More COF can create channels but also aggregation and brittle defects.
- More hydrophilicity supports Grotthuss hopping but may weaken mechanical stability.
- Fiber reinforcement can stabilize the membrane but may interrupt through-plane proton conduction.
- Low-RH performance often depends on fixed proton carriers, not only water uptake.

## Level 6: Evidence Hierarchy

1. Composition and structure are verified.
2. Morphology shows continuous, well-dispersed pathways.
3. Conductivity is measured across RH/T with clear EIS fitting.
4. Swelling, water uptake, IEC, and mechanics explain the conductivity.
5. Leaching and cycling tests show stability.
6. MEA data confirms practical fuel-cell relevance.

## Working Hypothesis Classes

- H1: Sulfonated COF grown on electrospun PAN inside PFSA improves conductivity while restraining swelling.
- H2: COF/ionic liquid/PVDF nanofibers enable high-temperature proton transport but risk liquid leaching.
- H3: Chemically bonded heteropoly-acid COF/Nafion improves low-RH performance by immobilizing acid clusters.
- H4: Continuous or supported SCOF membranes outperform filler-blend membranes by avoiding aggregation and channel discontinuity.
