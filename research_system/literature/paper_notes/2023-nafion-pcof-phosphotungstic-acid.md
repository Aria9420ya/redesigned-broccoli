# Paper Note: Nafion/P-COF Phosphotungstic-Acid Composite PEM

## Citation

High performance nanocomposite proton exchange membranes based on nanohybrids formed by chemically bonding phosphotungstic acid with COFs. Journal of Power Sources, 2023.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpowsour.2022.232332

## Why This Is A-Core

This is a key low-humidity strategy: phosphotungstic acid is chemically bound to COF to reduce leaching while retaining proton-conducting acid clusters.

## Core Claim

Chemically bonding phosphotungstic acid to COF creates a water-insoluble P-COF filler that improves Nafion membrane proton conductivity and fuel-cell performance under reduced humidity.

## Mechanism Chain

COF scaffold -> chemically anchored phosphotungstic acid -> fixed acid-rich proton sites -> better water/proton retention under low RH -> improved Nafion composite conductivity and MEA output.

## Key Reported Data

- Membrane type: Nafion/P-COF nanocomposite.
- Conductivity: 0.061 S/cm at 25 C and 55% RH.
- Fuel-cell peak power density: 724 mW/cm2 at 80 C and 50% RH.

## What To Extract Next

- Bonding chemistry between COF and phosphotungstic acid.
- P-COF loading series.
- Leaching test conditions.
- Water uptake and swelling compared with Nafion.
- MEA test gas, humidity, catalyst loading, and membrane thickness.

## Reproducibility Risks

- Physical adsorption could masquerade as chemical immobilization unless leaching evidence is strong.
- High filler loading may create interfacial defects.
- Nafion compatibility and dispersion quality are decisive.

## Use For Our Project

Use this as the benchmark for low-RH acid immobilization. Even if the first route is PAN/PFSA/sulfonated COF, this paper helps define how to prove acid sites are fixed and useful under low humidity.

## Next Experiment Idea

For any acid-loaded COF system, require a before/after soaking conductivity test plus elemental or spectroscopic evidence that the proton carrier remains in the membrane.
