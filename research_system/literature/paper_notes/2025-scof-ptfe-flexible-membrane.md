# Paper Note: SCOF/PTFE Flexible Proton Exchange Membrane

## Citation

Highly flexible SCOF proton exchange membrane reinforced with PTFE to enhance fuel cell power density. Journal of Membrane Science, 2025.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.memsci.2024.123479

## Why This Is A-Core

This is a frontier benchmark for moving beyond filler blending: the membrane uses a supported sulfonated COF phase to combine proton conduction with mechanical flexibility and high fuel-cell power density.

## Core Claim

Growing a sulfonated COF within a porous PTFE support can create a flexible, high-conductivity PEM with strong fuel-cell performance.

## Mechanism Chain

Porous PTFE support -> continuous BD(SO3H)2-COF phase -> dense sulfonic-acid proton pathway -> flexible reinforced membrane -> high MEA power output.

## Key Reported Data

- Conductivity: 249.2 mS/cm at 80 C.
- Fuel-cell peak power density: 1195.3 mW/cm2.
- Elongation at break: 124.4%.

## What To Extract Next

- Whether conductivity was measured at 100% RH or lower RH.
- COF growth method inside PTFE.
- Membrane thickness and area scalability.
- Gas crossover and durability.
- Comparison to Nafion under identical MEA conditions.

## Reproducibility Risks

- Continuous COF growth in support may be synthetically demanding.
- High power density may depend on optimized MEA conditions, not membrane alone.
- PTFE support compatibility and pore filling uniformity matter.

## Use For Our Project

Use as a long-term target and performance ceiling. It helps define what "high-level" looks like: continuous channels, mechanical support, and MEA validation.

## Next Experiment Idea

If PAN/PFSA/COF succeeds, consider whether a porous support or aligned fiber mat can be used to move from filler composite toward continuous COF-supported architecture.
