# Paper Note: PVDF/COF/Ionic-Liquid Nanofiber High-Temperature PEM

## Citation

Preparation of high temperature proton exchange membrane through covalent organic framework doped polyvinylidene fluoride nanofibers. International Journal of Hydrogen Energy, 2024.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhydene.2024.10.199

## Why This Is A-Core

This paper represents the high-temperature electrospun route: COF and ionic liquid are placed into PVDF nanofiber architecture to support proton transport beyond ordinary hydrated PEM conditions.

## Core Claim

COF-doped PVDF nanofibers containing ionic liquid can improve high-temperature PEM conductivity and fuel-cell power output.

## Mechanism Chain

PVDF electrospun nanofiber network -> COF porous/functional filler -> ionic liquid proton carrier -> high-temperature proton conduction pathway -> improved fuel-cell output at 100-120 C.

## Key Reported Data

- Membrane type: COF/ionic-liquid/PVDF nanofiber composite PEM.
- Peak power density: 178.8 mW/cm2 at 100 C.
- Peak power density: 326.5 mW/cm2 at 120 C.

## What To Extract Next

- Ionic liquid identity, loading, and immobilization evidence.
- COF identity, pore chemistry, and loading.
- PVDF spinning parameters.
- Conductivity versus temperature and humidity.
- Leaching or mass-loss test after soaking/heating.
- MEA operation humidity and durability.

## Reproducibility Risks

- Ionic-liquid leaching may create unstable performance.
- PVDF is not inherently proton-conductive, so pathway continuity depends on additives.
- High-temperature performance may be sensitive to membrane drying history.
- Conductivity improvement may not persist under long operation.

## Use For Our Project

Use this as a second route if the project shifts toward high-temperature PEMs. It should not replace the PAN/PFSA/sulfonated COF route unless high-temperature operation becomes the main goal.

## Next Experiment Idea

Before synthesizing a complex PVDF/COF/ionic-liquid membrane, design a leaching stress test: weigh, soak, dry, remeasure conductivity, and compare FTIR/XPS before and after soaking.
